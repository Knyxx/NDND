- The Ashwardens trace their origin to Ashhold, the ancestral heart of their order, nestled within the rugged spires of [[Stonehowe]] in southern Karnvald. They are an order of unwavering vigilance, called upon in times of greatest peril to protect the living from the restless dead. When such crises arise, the Ashwardens are led by the Ash King or Ash Queen, an elected sovereign whose wisdom and courage are deemed worthy to guide them through calamity. Such a leader is chosen only in the gravest of need, for the order thrives on discipline and collective purpose rather than monarchy.
- Pledges may be taken at any age, drawn by devotion, duty, or destiny. Most Ashwardens remain within the stone halls of Ashhold, where the air is thick with ash and the echoes of centuries past. Here, they dedicate themselves to pacifying the Restless within the [[Gravedeep]], navigating their labyrinthine tombs to bind spirits, quell unrest, and maintain the sacred balance between life and death. Yet many venture beyond, roaming [[Sundralith]] to tend to burial rites, guide travellers safely through perilous lands, and see that the dead are honoured as tradition demands.
- The people of [[Sundralith]] hold the Ashwardens in profound respect and implicit trust. Villagers leave doors open, offer hearth and table, and present coin or gift to acknowledge their tireless guardianship. It is said that to encounter an Ashwarden is to witness devotion incarnate, for each member pours every measure of their strength and spirit into service.
- The Ashwardens are unmistakable. Each bears ash across their forehead, a silent testament to their sacred calling. Their heavy armour is engraved with subtle runes of warding, and their weapons—ranging from colossal two-handed monsters to the paired discipline of a smaller weapon and tower shield—are crafted for endurance and precision. Among their most iconic tools are the Iron Chainwhip, tipped with a spiked mace head, and the Runic Lantern, carried only by fully trained members. The lantern’s light is said to pierce shadows and guide wandering souls, a symbol of the Ashwardens’ unbroken vigilance and solemn duty.
- To see an Ashwarden is to see the boundary between the living and the dead itself—a figure of ash and steel, relentless, solemn, and resolute. Their presence ensures that the restless are calmed, the living guided, and the ancient pact between the worlds of the living and the dead remains unbroken.
