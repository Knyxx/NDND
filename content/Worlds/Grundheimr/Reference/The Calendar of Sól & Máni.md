The northern peoples measure time by Sól (the Sun) and Máni (the Moon). Months follow the moon, while seasons follow the sun.

## Year Eras

- BC — Before Calamity
- CY — Years of Calamity
- AD — After Desolation

---
## Seasons (Two-Season Year)

- Sunseason (Months 1–6) — Bright half of the year
- Frostseason (Months 7–13) — Dark half of the year

---
## Year Structure

- 13 months per year
- 28 days per month
- 364 days per year
- Full Moon always falls on Day 14

---
## Lunar Cycle (28 Days)

- Day 1: New Moon
- Day 7: First Quarter
- Day 14: Full Moon
- Day 21: Last Quarter
- Day 28: Dark Moon

---

## Major Festivals

- Sunrise Feast — Glowmoon (1), Day 1
	- Purpose: Start of Sunseason, celebrating light and growth
	- Practices: Offerings to Sól and Máni, planting rites, feasts, small fire rituals
- Victory Feast — Glowmoon (1), Day 14
	- Purpose: Honors victory, courage, and oaths
	- Practices: Competitions, oaths of loyalty, offerings to Tyr or Odin
- High Sun Feast — Sunsreach (6), Day 14
	- Purpose: Height of summer, abundance, and prosperity
	- Practices: Outdoor feasts, music and dance, sun-facing rituals, blessings for crops
- Frostwake — Winterwake (9), Day 1
	- Purpose: Start of Frostseason, preparing for long nights
	- Practices: Ancestor veneration, hearth lighting, protective charms for home and kin
- Shieldmaidens’ Feast — Winterwake (9), Day 14
	- Purpose: Honors female spirits and protective deities, seeking fertility and guidance
	- Practices: Offerings of food, libations, gifts to local spirits or landwights
- Yulebright — Yulelight (11), Days 1–3
	- Purpose: Midwinter festival of family, ancestors, and light
	- Practices: Feasts, storytelling, lighting candles or bonfires, honoring deceased kin
- Thunder Feast — Thorrim (13), Day 14
	- Purpose: End-of-year festival, honoring Thor, endurance, and renewal
	- Practices: Meat feasts, drinking rituals, games of strength, offerings to Thor for protection
- Dark Night — Thorrim (13), Day 28
	- Purpose: Year’s end, reflection, and preparation for the new year
	- Practices: Silent reflection, offerings to spirits, planning for Sunseason

## Date Format

Dates are typically written as:

[Weekday], Day [X], [Month], [Year] [Era]
Example: Thor’s Day, Day 14, Ylir, 12 AD
