The fires of the forge had always been meant for [[Zamos]].

He had been born between two powerful clans, raised beneath banners heavy with history, taught from his first breath that strength and honor were his inheritance. The certainty of that life had followed him until the day he stood before the ancestral blade, the gathered clan watching in silence.

The flame did not come.

His hand trembled. The blade remained cold.

No decree was spoken, yet judgment settled over the hall like ash. The elders did not condemn him; they did not need to. Warriors shifted away, creating space where reverence once stood. His father’s gaze lingered only a moment before turning aside, bound by law, pride, and the weight of expectation. Silence became law. Distance became punishment.

Only Gaia moved toward him.

His mother’s hand found his shoulder, steady and unyielding. That night, and in the days that followed, she spoke to him of the old laws—those written before ceremony and title, before fire decided worth. She told him stories the clan had forgotten, of honour measured by choice rather than blood. She stood beside him openly, and in doing so, stood against her husband’s silence.

At her urging, Zamos laid aside privilege and took up the hammer.

The smithy became his world. Heat rolled over him in waves. Iron screamed beneath the strike. Sweat burned his eyes and soaked his skin. There, among fire and labour, he learned patience where pride had failed him, restraint where anger threatened to rise. Steel did not care who he was—only how true his hand fell.

It was there that he met the Orc, Gromash.

Once a warrior of another clan, Gromash now worked under guard, a prisoner in all but name. Yet when he spoke, his voice carried no bitterness. He spoke of brothers who had stood shoulder to shoulder, shields locked, blood shared in defence of what mattered. Of loyalty tested not by words, but by survival. Zamos listened as iron cooled and sparks faded, and in those stories heard the echo of the ideals his own people claimed to serve.

When the time came for Zamos to leave and seek his own path, a decision tore at him.

To walk away from a man who still lived by those ideals felt like a betrayal greater than any law. Under cover of night, Zamos unlocked Gromash’s chains and watched him slip into darkness, convinced he would never see him again.

He returned to his chamber to gather what little he would carry.

The fortress erupted.

Fire raced through the halls. Steel rang against stone. Smoke poured through corridors, thick and choking. Zamos ran, calling for his mother, heart pounding as the world he had known collapsed around him.

He found her on the cold stone floor, her body cut open, blood spreading beneath her like a dark shadow. The sound left him. The world narrowed to that single, unbearable truth: the one person who had never turned away from him was gone.

A shout tore through the chaos.

Zamos ran.

On the outer wall, he found Gromash—bloodied, weapon in hand—standing where he should have been long gone. Their eyes locked. Gromash lifted a free hand, not in threat, but in plea. His mouth shaped a single word.

_Wait._

Zamos never heard what came after.

A crushing blow struck his back. Stone vanished beneath his feet. Through pain and smoke, he saw Gromash lunge forward—but whether in defence, betrayal, or desperation, he would never know. Darkness swallowed him as he fell.

He wakes unable to move.

Pain pins him to the earth, every breath a blade in his chest. Above him, the sky is choked with smoke, turning dawn into a dull, bleeding grey. He can only watch as the fortress burns.

Stone cracks. Towers collapse inward. Roofs give way in thunderous sighs. Banners blacken, curl, and fall. The home of his ancestors dies piece by piece, and he is forced to witness every moment of it.

The clan is gone. His mother is dead.

Ash drifts down, settling across his face and armour like a funeral shroud. The heat dries the blood on his skin. Somewhere, far beyond the fire, the last screams fade into silence.

Helpless, broken, and burning with grief, Zamos makes his vow.

Not of blind vengeance.  
Not of wrath.

Justice.

He will learn who orchestrated the slaughter. He will uncover why Gromash had still been there when the fortress burned. And he will determine whether the blood on that blade marked true guilt—or a failure born of mercy, mirroring his own.

The smoke thins.

For a single, fragile moment, the clouds part.

Sunlight breaks through the ash, pale and unwavering, spilling across the ruin. At the same time, a sliver of moon hangs impossibly above the horizon—faint, distant, yet present. Light and shadow fall together across the shattered stone, crossing over Zamos where he lies.

He feels it then.

Not a voice.  
Not a command.

A weight. A balance. As if something ancient has taken notice—not to judge, not to comfort, but to witness.

Then hands grasp his shoulders.

Voices cut through the haze. Men from a nearby village pull him from the rubble as the last of the fortress collapses behind them. The light fades. The sky closes. The moment passes.

But it is not forgotten.

Days blur into weeks as his body mends. While he heals, Zamos listens. He watches. From scorched records salvaged from the ruins and whispered fears drawn from trembling mouths, he begins to piece together the truth. This was no random raid. No accident of war.

It was planned.

The trail leads outward—toward the Crossroads Encampment.

When Zamos finally rises and turns away from the ashes of his past, his oath weighs heavier than any blade. Mercy has shown its cost. Justice now demands its due.

Behind him, the ruins smolder in silence.

Ahead, the road waits.

And though the gods have spoken no words, Zamos knows—his oath has been seen.
