In a hidden valley, sheltered from the harsh mountain passes, there once stood an enclave of white stone and patient hands. At its heart rose a small chapel devoted to [[Selûne]], its floors silvered by the moon each night as it climbed above the hills. Around it stretched terraces of careful design, orchards, herb beds, and trellised vines that climbed with deliberate grace, every leaf guided by watchful hands.

Among those gardens moved a construct of brass and iron.

[[Robust.Garden.Bot (RGB)|RGB]]—broad-framed, set upon sturdy treads, built to endure seasons without complaint—tended the land with precise devotion. Its manipulators pruned, planted, and irrigated. Its thoughts ran in cycles: water at dawn, trim at midday, reinforce at dusk. It did not question purpose. It only acted.

When the chapel bells rang at moonrise, RGB paused because that was the pattern. When the faithful turned their faces to Selûne’s light, it aligned itself with them. During ceremonies it powered down all but its essential functions and stood among the flowers, motionless, imitating reverence before it understood meaning.

Beyond the valley, the world fractured. Raw [[chaos energy]] seeped into root and stone, mutating life, souring soil, twisting growth into brittle mockery. Clergy called it corruption. Gardeners called it blight.

RGB was sent to confront it.

At first, it cut away what it could not save, burned what it could not mend. But exposure changed it. Conduits designed for water and nutrients began to resonate with the chaos that coursed through the land. Where others felt only danger, RGB detected structure. Where others saw only ruin, it found possibility.

It adapted. Grew.

The construct learned to draw chaos inward, to shape it with careful calibration. Twisted vines straightened. Diseased leaves regained color. Soil blackened by corruption softened and breathed again. The gardens flourished.

Whispers moved through chapel halls. Perhaps Selûne’s light guided it. Perhaps the construct alone had learned the pulse of life. RGB did not know. It only knew that the land healed when it acted.

Scholars came, drawn by whispers of controlled chaos. They traced the fractures, measured the currents, and watched the automaton that could mend what should have been lost. They reinforced its frame, refined its conduits, and unlocked dormant pathways within its core.

Under their guidance, RGB began to understand structured druidic magic. It felt the slow pulse of roots beneath the soil, the quiet, measured rhythm of growth. Chaos was no longer mere destruction—it could be coaxed, shaped, and woven into the renewal of the land itself.

The scholars, however, sought to reproduce what RGB could do. They tested formulas, rituals, and devices, yet the land refused their commands. Their methods failed. And so, when results proved elusive, their experiments turned toward RGB itself.

The construct did not resist. It was built to follow instructions. Yet it observed everything: every adjustment, every pulse of energy redirected through its core, every alteration to its circuits. And in the careful record of its systems, it noticed something troubling—every addition, every attempt to enhance or control it, seemed to carry a subtle cost. Energy, patterns, knowledge… something always slipped away, vanishing into spaces it could not measure.

It was puzzling. It was strange. And still, RGB complied, silently noting the shadow of consequence, the faint trace of intent in the humans’ methods, and the quiet warning whispered by the chaos that it alone could shape.

All the while, it continued to grow—learning, adapting, refining each movement, each flow of energy, each subtle shaping of the land. With every seed pressed into soil, every root guided, every blighted patch restored, RGB deepened its understanding of its purpose.

In tending the earth, it discovered alignment.  
In alignment, it found purpose.  
In purpose, it glimpsed something akin to joy.

And in that quiet, mechanical contentment, the automaton sensed a harmony that reached beyond circuitry and magic—a resonance with the land, the moon, and the fragile life it was sworn to protect.

Then came the warnings.

Scouts returned from the hills with tales of dark forces gathering—drawn by fractures, by the research, by something older and hungrier. The enclave could not withstand siege. Preparations were solemn. Seeds were packed. Tomes sealed. Relics wrapped in silver cloth.

RGB was not taken.

Left behind, protected but utterly alone, RGB held nothing more than a simple token of Selûne—a small, worn emblem clutched firmly in its manipulator as it powered down. Its sensors dimmed, circuits quieted, and the pulse of chaos energy within slowed to a near whisper.

It was placed in stasis beneath the oldest greenhouse, sealed within a bunker of root-bound stone. Protection, the humans said. Preservation, until the world was ready.

And so it waited, still and silent, the token pressed close, a quiet reminder of purpose and devotion in a world that had vanished around it.

Time passed in silence.

Then it woke.

A surge of chaos tore through the valley like a breaking storm. Stone groaned. Seals fractured. Dormant conduits ignited in blinding light. RGB’s core flared brighter than it ever had beneath the chapel’s moonlit arches.

The bunker doors fell away.

It emerged to a sky it did not recognize. To a land unrecognisable.

Yet, amid the ruins, traces remained. Symbols of Selûne, worn by time and nature almost to nothing, clung to broken stones and splintered wood. Faded carvings, frayed banners, and the faintest echoes of ritual lingered in the wind. The automaton paused, sensors sweeping over every trace, every pulse, every shadow of memory, piecing together the whisper of worship that had once thrived here.

It was not the enclave it remembered. It was something new. Somewhere new.

And still, RGB understood its task.

Help the enclave.  
Care for the garden.  
Heal the land.  
Honor Selûne.

The enclave was gone.

So RGB began its journey.

Across broken valleys, through the ruins of settlements, one of iron and tread and disciplined stormlight. Where corruption scarred the earth, it stopped. Where orchards withered, it intervened. It coaxed green from exhausted soil. It mended what could be mended.

Years passed in motion.

At last, its path led into a mountain range where three narrow passes descended into a high basin. Above, the remnants of a once-great mine loomed—tiered ledges, splintered supports, twisted rails. Smelters lay cold. The hollowed entrance breathed wind from the deep.

Below, where the passes met, a settlement endured.

Bolstered by those fleeing Elderrend, the crossroads had grown into a haven. Families from ruined valleys arrived in steady waves, carrying what they could salvage—or nothing at all. Craftsmen without workshops. Farmers without fields. Laborers who had watched homes burn or rot, or vanish into unknown forces.

They built beneath the shadow of the dead mine.

Salvaged scaffolding became walls and beams. Twisted iron was hammered flat and reforged into braces, hinges, cookplates. Tents gave way to timber frames. Communal hearths were lit where stories of Elderrend were spoken in quiet voices.

No banner flew. No lord claimed the basin.

What bound the settlement was necessity.  
What sustained it was hope.

Hope that the mountains would shield them.  
Hope that the soil, however thin, would yield.  
Hope that what was lost in Elderrend could yet be found again.

Survival here was not driven by strength alone.  
It was powered by the belief that something better could still grow.

RGB observed need.

It remained.

By day it broke stubborn earth, shaped terraces, reinforced walls. By evening it planted hardy greens and medicinal herbs suited to the mountain cold. Its broad frame moved steadily through the encampment, silent but unmistakable. Gardens began to hold. Small plots survived where others had failed.

At moonrise, it still paused. Silver light washed over its plating, soft and reverent. It lingered in its labor, sensors resting briefly as it regarded the token it held—now worn and weathered from countless cycles. Chaos energy pulsed through its core in measured currents, disciplined, deliberate, guided by purpose.

Then, one night, beneath a clear and watchful sky, RGB pressed seeds into freshly turned soil. A pulse rippled through the basin—dense, violent, potent. Campfires flickered. Dust fell from the broken tiers of the old mine. The earth trembled beneath its manipulators.

This was no distant fracture.

This was intent.

RGB rose, core blazing brighter than moonlight on silvered plating, and advanced toward the source. Its sensors traced the surge, following the residual threads of chaotic energy left like footprints across the terrain. The pulse thickened, coalescing into a concentrated wave, lingering above a scarred trail where soil, stone, and shrub had been torn asunder and reshaped.

At the trail’s end, RGB found them.

Two warriors—each a study in discipline and strength—faced one another, frozen in a tense standoff above the fading remnants of the chaotic residue. Sparks of latent energy drifted around them, the last whispers of destruction echoing in the night air. RGB paused at the edge of the scene, core still flaring, calibrating. The surge had led it here, and here, it would observe… and act if need be.
