#### Approaching the Clan Hold

###### DM READ

The road rises, winding through parched grass and scrub. The ruins of a stronghold appear jagged against the sky.

A gate dominates the fractured wall. One massive door lies face-down in the weeds, split, rotting, splintered. The other tilts on rusted hinges, groaning with the wind. The ditch before the wall is half-choked with soil and creeping vines. Jagged palisade stakes have rotted to splintered stumps. A faint tang of rust and decay hangs in the air.

**GAR-RUK READ**

I remember the gate as it once was — towering and proud. The warriors at their posts offered half-nods and sly grins, already anticipating the stories of mischief that would reach the longhouse by nightfall. Below, the ditch teemed with the muddy feet of clan children, running and shouting, their joy clear and unbroken.


#### Entering the Clan Hold

**DM READ**

The courtyard stretches beyond the gate, littered with broken beams and shattered stone. Roofs have collapsed, some slabs hanging precariously, swaying slightly with the wind. Walls lean and crack, scarred by time and violence. Sparse tufts of grass and scraggly weeds push stubbornly through the cobbles, while ivy and moss cling in sheltered corners, slowly claiming the ruin. Loose stones clatter under gusts of wind, their echoes sharp and hollow. A dry, rattling whistle threads through the gaps in the walls, carrying the faint scent of dust, dry timber, and rusted iron.

Across the courtyard, the longhouse endures — the only structure still worthy of being called a building. Its stone foundation is solid, roughly hewn yet steadfast, while massive iron-reinforced beams brace the sagging roof where timber elsewhere has failed. Broad and low, it favors strength over elegance, its carved wooden supports etched with crude, purposeful markings — trophies of hunts, victories, and clan pride. Even in ruin, it exudes the orcish sense of practicality and endurance, a fortress of hearth and hall amid the shattered courtyard.


**GAR-RUK READ**

I hear voices echoing across the yard, laughter mingling with the steady thrum of wagons over the cobbles and the rhythm of axes striking wood. The air smells of fresh timber, smoke, and the faint tang of earth. Inside the longhouse, the hearth blazes, warmth spilling into the hall as the clan gathers for the evening meal. Children press forward, eager to greet the returning hunters, their eyes bright, hoping to be the first to hear the new stories that will fill the night.


**DM PROMPT**
A warm glow spills from gaps in the walls, flickering across the ruin, casting restless, jittering shadows that seem to move of their own accord.


#### Introducing the Hunter

The longhouse interior is littered with rubble — splintered beams, shattered boards, shards of stone, and overturned chairs and tables scattered across the floor. The walls lean and sag where timbers have given way, and gaps in the roof allow flickers of light to fall across the debris. At the center, the old fire pit sits cold and blackened, a great horn suspended by chains above it, its etched surface worn and scarred by time.

In one corner, a lean-to shelters a small fire. A pot simmers, sending rich, savory aromas curling into the hall, while meat skewers drip fat onto the coals, hissing and smoking. Shadows stretch and flicker across the walls and scattered furniture, crawling over broken timbers, overturned stools, and jagged stones, it's movement a caricature of life.

By the fire, a hooded figure sits, cloak drawn low, shoulders hunched yet poised with quiet authority. Six old, scarred hunting dogs lie around him, their bodies coiled but relaxed, eyes sharp and unblinking, tracking your every movement.

Pale yellow eyes flick open beneath the hood, catching the firelight. They sweep over you once, sharp and wary.

“Who might you be… more scavengers? Be gone from this dead place.”

His gaze slides back to the simmering pot, dismissive, the dogs shifting but silent around him.

**Optional Insight (DC 12–14)**: The hunter is tired and wary. Relying on his dogs more than himself right now.


#### Dealing with the Hunter

1. **Leave Immediately**
	- Outcome: The party can retreat safely; neither Hunter nor hunting pack pursue.

2. **Attempt Dialogue / Reason**
	- **Skill Checks**: Persuasion, Insight, or Deception (DC 14–16).
	- Success: The Hunter speaks, low and deliberate:
		- Persuasion: I lived here once… long before this ruin. The brothers could never agree. One left to keep the peace… I was sent to bring him home. I could not. When I returned… all that remained was this.
		- Insight: He sits still, pale eyes flicking briefly over you, measuring. He trusts the dogs to watch first, holding back, restrained, distant.
		- Deception: Hmph… perhaps. Keep your distance, then. I do not linger here often. This place… holds memories better left alone.
	- Failure: The Hunter speaks, firmly:
		- “Be gone. Now.”
		- The Hunter sits up and some dogs stand and stretch.

3. Press Forward / Aggressive
	- The Hunter whistles sharply, a crisp, commanding sound.
	- The dogs spring to surround the party, low growls vibrating through the hall.
	- The Hunter disappears into cover, eyes watching, ready to strike.
	- Combat begins, immediate and coordinated with the dogs.

#### The Horn of Grusk Oxfell

The Hunter staggers, clutching his wounds, hood slipping back. At last, he sees Gar-ruk clearly, and his eyes flare with recognition.

“Another… maybe Grusk’s spirit hasn’t left these lands,” he rasps.

He sways, almost collapsing, then whistles sharply. The dogs, confused for a heartbeat, surge to his side — loyal, protective, alert.

With a trembling hand, The Hunter gestures toward one of the pillars. “My breath… near gone… just one thing left,” he gasps.

He limps forward and releases the crank; the horn descends, hovering just above the fire pit.

“Do what I could not… take the horn… let the hills hear us… let them know Oxfell endures.”

His strength fails, and he collapses toward the horn, eyes glazing as the hall falls silent. The dogs whimper softly, pressed close at his side.

**Player's Choice**

1. Gar-ruk refuses to blow the horn
	- The Hunter’s pale eyes narrow. A low, rasping curse slips from his lips, heavy with anger and disappointment.
	- "Oxfell’s blood is spent… may your weakness die with you, and no more children be burdened with our name."
	- Whistles. Dogs attack Gar-ruk. Hunter dies.

2. Gar-ruk Blows the Horn
	- A massive, echoing blast tears through the valley, rolling over hills and shattered trees. The sound vibrates through the party’s bones, deep and resonant.
	- The party feels a surge of strength.
	- A bestial yell answers. Shadows twist and coalesce.
	- A spectral minotaur emerges, eyes glowing, horns lowered. With terrifying speed, it descends upon The Hunter, executing him where he stands. The hall shakes with the impact. and dogs attack the Minotaur.
	- The party now faces the minotaur, their temporary boon the only shield against its wrath.

#### Finding the Key

Any attempt to lift the horn or free it from its chains sends it tumbling into the fire pit, where it cracks against the stone. Within the fractured shell, something is fixed in place: an ancient runic seal, impeccably preserved, thrumming with a quiet, potent energy.
