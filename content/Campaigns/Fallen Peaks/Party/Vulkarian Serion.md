<!-- DM-ONLY

I wasn't born into a traveling troupe. I was a Junior Scribe at the Great Athenaeum, a massive library in a sprawling city. My job was mundane: transcribing old taxes and trade routes.

However, I have a condition (or a gift): I can hear the echoes of history in objects. One day, while cleaning a dusty, forgotten wing, I found a Fragment, a literal piece of a shattered celestial tablet. When I touched it, I didn't see a god; I saw a conspiracy. I realised that the current history books are lies, written by the winners of a war no one remembers.

When I tried to tell the High Archivists, they laughed at me. When I persisted, they fired me and burned my research!! I didn't just leave; I was evicted. Now, I travel the world not because I want to perform, but because I am the only one who knows the real story, and I won't stop talking about it until someone listens.

I am writing a book, the decoupling of truth, Every dungeon I clear and every monster the party kills is just another chapter for my masterpiece. I will often stop in the middle of a fight to ask a goblin about its "cultural heritage" so I can record it accurately.

"Oh, the readers are never going to believe this chapter."

Validation: I have a massive chip on your shoulder. I want to become so famous that the Great Athenaeum has no choice but to invite me back and admit I was right.

The Truth-Seeker: I hate secrets. If an NPC is acting suspicious, I won't just ignore it, I'll use my high Charisma and spells to pry the truth out of them, often to the party’s embarrassment.

DM stuff

1. What do we notice first?
	1. The Ink-Stained Aristocrat: At first glance, he looks like a refined scholar, but the cuffs of his fine tunic are permanently stained with ink.
	2. The "Frantic" Look: He likely has a slightly wild look in his eyes, less madman and more "man who has seen the truth and can't believe you haven't."
	3. The Journal: He’s almost certainly clutching The Decoupling of Truth or has it strapped to his chest like it's worth more than his life (which, to him, it is).
2. Shaping Moments
	1. The Eviction: The day the High Librarians burned his research and physically threw him out of the Great Athenaeum. This shaped his distrust of official history and his need to find an audience that will actually listen.
	2. The First Performance: The moment he realized he could hide his dangerous truths inside songs. Realizing that people listen to a melody more than a lecture was his "aha!" moment for becoming a Bard.
	3. The Silent Gods: That specific night he spent in the ruins of an old temple, proving to himself through star-charts and ancient texts that the gods had truly "decoupled." That's when his obsession became his identity.
3. Past Interactions Ideas
	1. Mamma Bear (Rogue Shifter): Perhaps Vuk hired her to "acquire" a restricted tome from a private collection. He provided the maps; she provided the muscle.
		1. The Debt: Maybe she saved him from a street brawl in a rough part of town when he was trying to "educate" the local drunkards about the gods.
	2. Cal Copperpocket (Red Dwarf with a Spork): As a scholar, Vuk might have been fascinated by Cal’s unique physiology or his... choice of weaponry. He might have shadowed Cal for a week, taking notes on "Alternative Dwarven Culinary Combat," much to Cal's annoyance.
		1. The Tavern Connection: They might have shared a bench at a roadside inn where Vuk traded a song for a bite of whatever Cal was eating with that spork.
	3. Grog (Half-Orc Cleric): This is the juiciest one. As a Cleric, Grog represents the very thing Vuk is investigating. They might have met at a temple where Vuk was trying to "interview" the clergy, and Grog was the only one patient enough not to kick him out immediately.
		1. Mutual Outcasts: They both stand out in a crowd. They might have bonded over the shared experience of being "too much" for polite society.
