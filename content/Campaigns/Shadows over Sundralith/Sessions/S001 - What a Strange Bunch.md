Beneath the cold shoulders of [[Varkundr]], the [[Crossroads Encampment]] settled toward night.

Fires were lit early to combat the dark. Wagons circled tight. Parents called children in with sharp voices that expected obedience. The mountains did not forgive carelessness after dark.

[[Gar-ruk Oxfell]] stood at the edge of the activity, broad and road-worn, dust still clinging to his boots and shoulders. He chewed the last of a grilled skewer and watched the camp with the detached caution of someone who had survived by not assuming welcome.

He preferred higher ground.

A small rise north of the main buildings offered visibility and stone enough at his back to sleep defensibly. He had just begun to measure the slope when the crash came.

Not the groan of timber giving way.

An impact.

Hard. Violent. Close.

Camp noise faltered but did not panic — people here were disciplined. [[Gar-ruk Oxfell|Gar-ruk]] moved first, circling the hill rather than cresting it.

The rear wall of a warehouse had been blown outward. Stone blocks lay scattered downslope in a crude trail of destruction that led to an old burial cairn long absorbed into the hillside.

Its entrance stood open.

[[Gar-ruk Oxfell|Gar-ruk]] did not step inside.

A voice stopped him first.

“Explain.”

From the shadow between stone and failing light stepped [[Zamos]], broad as a gate and encased in armour etched with the marks of his faith. His posture did not suggest inquiry. It suggested judgment.

Suspicion was not personal. It was survival.

[[Gar-ruk Oxfell|Gar-ruk]] answered evenly. Neither man lowered their guard.

They were not alone for long.

Drawn by a pulse that did not belong in cultivated soil, [[RGB]] left their garden patch without hesitation. Moving with mechanical purpose toward the disturbance, unaware they were being tracked.

At a distance, careful and observant, followed [[Elara Ashbud]]. The spirit housed within her [[Ashbud’s Spirit Lantern|lantern]] had reacted sharply — not violently, but distinctly. That alone was enough reason to investigate.

Four strangers now stood before a broken grave.

Introductions began in fragments. Names. Origins. Little else.

A soft cough drifted from within the cairn.

Weapons cleared sheaths without discussion.

The interior was damp and stale, the air long sealed. Water traced narrow paths down the stone. Their steps echoed too loudly.

Only a few paces in, they found a black-haired elf slumped against the curved wall.

[[Shimmer]] blinked at them as though surfacing from a dream. Disoriented, but not afraid. Her gaze fixed on [[RGB|RGB]] with particular intensity.

She reached out and took one metal finger between her own, testing weight and reality as if confirming a theory.

Glass shattered deeper within.

This time, no one hesitated.

They pressed forward into the larger chamber where pooled water reflected the lantern light in trembling distortions.

A figure stood within it.

Red-scaled. Wiry. Tall. Steam lifting faintly where warmer skin met mountain chill. One arm was blackened from tip to elbow, not from flame but as though dipped in ink.

He drew a long breath through his nose and cleared it sharply.

This was not his first miscalculation.

Magical travel was rarely as precise as its architects claimed. He had been displaced before — thrown wide of intention, cast into foreign geographies by instability in the weave.

Annoying.

Painful.

Survivable.

He examined the chamber, the stonework, the strangers with weapons half-raised.

Dragonborn were rare in these lands, but not unheard of. He saw recognition in their eyes. Suspicion, yes — but not disbelief.

Good.

That simplified things.

He flexed his blackened arm once, assessing damage. Functional.

Then he looked at them fully.

“I require trousers.”

The mountains did not answer.

The cairn remained open.

And six strangers, drawn by accident or design, stood together in the damp dark — not yet aware that this small convergence would bind their paths more tightly than any of them intended.
