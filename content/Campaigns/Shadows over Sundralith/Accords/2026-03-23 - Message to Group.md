### Limits

No new limits raised, I've refined those raised previously to be more in line with how we've played and discussed.

- Absolute no: Sexual assault and drugging for the purpose of.
- Off-screen only: Severe mental distress

This means that anything else can happen, though it does not mean it will.

Examples:
- Kids in last session could have drowned off-screen but would not drown while a PC was aware of them.
- People can be poisoned without knowledge, will roll saves with disadvantage.

---

### Scope

Requested content was everything (Combat, Roleplay, Exploration, Mystery), dealing with situations appropriately will lead to more success, dealing with them inappropriately will lead to more consequences.

Examples:
- Goldi was a lost spirit, by dealing with her as a person and settling her body to rest away from her the party gained maximum EXP from her and avoided a powerful enemy.
- Durough was a very powerful enemy, by attacking him head on the party risked being beaten severely, but changing tactics allowed them to escape.
- The party met an old man but did not investigate well, there may be more unknown.

---

### Clarity

Some things, once learnt by one character, might as well be known by all, others like mysteries or pieces learnt while away from the party, should not be known to all unless shared by those that know it.

- Enemy details
	- AC, once beaten, shown to players
	- Damage applied, shown to players
	- Bloody (50% HP), told to players
- While away from scene, players will be fully muted

### Gameplay

The world is built for the characters; the game is for the players.

There’s no single “right” way to play, but no one playstyle should dominate the table.

Your character can act freely, but shouldn’t undermine the party’s goals or take spotlight from others.

Most play happens from the PC point of view: Speaking in character or declaring intent (e.g. “Bob would do X”)

Above-table talk is encouraged when it helps:
- Clarifying plans
- Supporting another player
- Keeping the game moving

Avoid interrupting active PC/NPC moments unless it adds to the scene.

There will also be moments where the table naturally steps out of the scene to laugh, react, or enjoy something unexpected - that’s part of the game too - just let the moment pass, then return focus so everyone stays engaged.

Examples:
1. RP
	- Player A
		- (in character): “We’re new in town. Heard there’s been trouble on the northern road?”
		- (Out of character) “Trying to seem friendly and get more information.”
	- DM: “Roll Persuasion.”
	- Player B & C (above table):
		- “We should support this—keep the story consistent.”
		- “Yeah, don’t mention the cult yet.”
	- Player B (in character): Slides a drink over — “First round’s on us. Things have been rough, yeah?”
	- DM: “Nice, take advantage on the roll.”
- 2. Meta
	- DM: “The tunnel splits left and right.”
	- Player A (above table): “We know left had traps earlier.”
	- Player B (above table): “Yeah, but our characters might not remember that clearly.”
	- Player C (decision): “Let’s play it safe, but not perfectly—stick together and check for traps.”

### Cheat Sheets

Hesitation can add unnecessary pressure and delays when trying to decide the best/perfect action.

Before the next session, have 2–3 default actions your character would take:
- In combat
- In social situations
- In exploration/problem solving

These are your “go-to” moves when unsure. I’m happy to help you set these up.

Examples:
- Aggressive character:
	- Combat: Attack nearest enemy; hold the line in serious fights
	- Social: Intimidate or speak bluntly
	- Exploration: Push forward, trigger or force outcomes
- Tactical character:
	- Combat: Group enemies; use area effects; support when low on resources
	- Social: Gather information first; stay measured
	- Exploration: Scout, plan, avoid unnecessary risk
- Cautious character:
	- Combat: Stay back; protect self or allies
	- Social: Let others lead; observe
	- Exploration: Check for traps; move slowly and safely

### Combat

Sessions are far apart and we get caught by details and drag things on.

New combat approach, same steps but walked together, less time waste by sticking to timers.

During combat, flip the perspective, work together above the table plainly stating goals and plans each round, duck back into character to execute actions.

Example:
1. Enter combat, roll initiative
2. Above the table, confirm overall goal and roles (2 min)
	1. Kill everything, save hostage, escape, etc.
	2. PC1 will tank in this space, PC2 will shoot from up there...
	3. Priorities
3. Per round, some above the table talk
4. Per turn, players decide what their PC will do
5. If unsure, use cheat sheet or declare intent

I’ll prompt so we keep things moving, if taking too long to start acting, will default to Dodge action (until the start of your next turn, any attack roll made against you has Disadvantage if you can see the attacker, and you make Dexterity saving throws with Advantage. You lose these benefits if you have the Incapacitated condition or if your Speed is 0.).
